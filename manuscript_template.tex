\begin{abstract}
I present a hidden Markov model framework for longitudinal Crohn's disease monitoring with state-dependent laboratory sampling. The model combines daily Gaussian wearable summaries, intermittent log-normal laboratory biomarkers, and the laboratory-draw indicator in one likelihood; laboratory values contribute only on draw days. At each landmark day, I distinguish an unconditional first-passage distribution with current-flare mass at day zero from a transient-conditional distribution whose mean, for any fixed parameter set, is bounded by that set's Remission- and Mild-state hitting-time means. Hidden Markov model parameters are estimated without simulator state labels by Baum--Welch expectation maximization. A @@N_PARAM_MEMBERS@@-member responsibility-conditioned empirical-Bayes mixture propagates parameter variation; the event-history comparator uses a separate @@N_HAZARD_MEMBERS@@-member patient-bootstrap ensemble. The censored log score retains administratively censored landmarks and the probability tail beyond the observation window; fixed-horizon Brier scores, calibration, coverage, and sharpness complete the evaluation, with seed- and patient-clustered uncertainty. Across @@N_SEEDS_WORD@@ independent simulations of @@N_TOTAL@@ patients (@@N_TEST@@ held-out test patients each), the endogenous-sampling hidden Markov model obtained a censored log score (lower is better) of @@PROP_NLL@@ (95\% confidence interval @@PROP_NLL_CI@@), a four-horizon mean Brier score of @@PROP_IBS@@ (@@PROP_IBS_CI@@), and @@PROP_COV@@ coverage for nominal 90\% intervals. Comparator-minus-proposed differences for the otherwise identical model without the draw indicator were @@DIFF_NOLAM_NLL@@ in log score (@@DIFF_NOLAM_NLL_CI_ABS@@) and @@DIFF_NOLAM_IBS@@ in mean Brier score (@@DIFF_NOLAM_IBS_CI_ABS@@). The time-to-flare interface can be estimated without oracle states and evaluated end to end, but the analysis does not establish a practically important gain from the draw indicator in this well-specified regime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{rohn's} disease follows a relapsing--remitting course, idealized here as transitions among clinically quiescent, mildly active, and flare states. Wearable devices can provide dense physiological measurements, whereas laboratory biomarkers are more direct measures of systemic inflammation but are available only intermittently. The timing of a laboratory panel can itself be informative: patients with greater disease activity may be sampled more often~\cite{lange2015}. A monitoring model can therefore use the laboratory-draw indicator (whether a panel was drawn that day) as an observation rather than treating every absent laboratory value as missing at random.

A clinician-facing output should also be more explicit than a per-day flare score. At study day $d$, the relevant quantity is a distribution over the waiting time until the flare state is first occupied on or after day $d$. This waiting time is zero for a current flare; it is not the time to a new flare episode after recovery. A distinction arises when the filtered state posterior assigns positive probability to the flare state. The unconditional waiting-time law contains an atom at zero; the law conditional on not currently being in flare does not. Their means differ whenever the flare posterior is strictly between zero and one, and, for any fixed parameter set, only the latter is constrained to lie between that set's state-specific transient hitting-time means.

This study makes five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It specifies one coherent likelihood in which daily wearable observations are Gaussian, laboratory biomarkers are log-normal when observed, and absent laboratory values never enter the emission density.
\item It states, using standard absorbing-chain identities, both the unconditional and transient-conditional first-passage distributions (Section~\ref{sec:hitting}), including the day-zero mass and the finite-horizon survival tail.
\item It estimates the hidden Markov model (HMM) parameters by expectation maximization (EM) without using the simulator's true states, and it propagates parameter variation by a transparent responsibility-conditioned, equally weighted parameter mixture.
\item It compares the endogenous-sampling HMM with a no-draw HMM, a narrowly defined draw-stratified-emission HMM, and a supervised discrete-time event-history model trained with simulator flare labels, on the same time-to-flare output using censoring-aware proper scores and clustered uncertainty.
\item It supplies a one-command reproducibility pipeline, complete probability mass function (PMF) archives, serialized HMM and event-history ensembles, generated tables and figures, acceptance tests of the core invariants, and a verification script that regenerates the reported result tables from the archived patient- and landmark-level outputs.
\end{enumerate}

The scope is deliberately limited to a controlled simulation study. No claim of clinical validation is made.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Informative observation and dropout}
The distinction among missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR), and missing not at random (MNAR) is foundational in longitudinal analysis \cite{little_rubin,daniels_hogan}. Diggle and Kenward gave an early selection-model treatment of informative dropout in longitudinal data \cite{diggle}. Informative dropout models commonly connect a longitudinal process and an event process through shared latent structure. Bartolucci and Farcomeni developed a discrete-time event-history extension of latent Markov models with informative dropout \cite{bartolucci2015}, and later a shared-parameter continuous-time hidden Markov and survival model \cite{bartolucci2019}. Marino and Alf\`o used finite mixtures of hidden Markov models to accommodate longitudinal responses subject to dropout \cite{marino2020}. Pandolfi, Bartolucci, and Pennoni considered a continuous-response HMM that accommodates intermittent missingness under a MAR assumption and informative dropout through an absorbing state \cite{pandolfi2023}. The latent Markov works~\cite{bartolucci2015,bartolucci2019,marino2020,pandolfi2023} are directly relevant: they show how dropout processes can share latent structure with the longitudinal outcome and how EM recursions can estimate the resulting models. Speekenbrink and Visser treated state-dependent missingness in hidden Markov models directly, letting the probability that an observation is missing depend on the hidden state so that missingness itself carries information about the state \cite{speekenbrink2025}; the state-dependent draw probability $\lambda_i$ used here (Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}) is an instance of that construction, applied to daily laboratory-panel occurrence alongside wearable channels observed every day. Lange \emph{et al.}\ modeled a multistate disease process jointly with informative observation times, in which the timing of clinical encounters depends on the disease process \cite{lange2015}. State-dependent missingness is therefore not claimed here as a new contribution. The target of the present framework is a rolling first-passage prediction over time to flare, obtained by propagating the filtered state posterior through the transient block of the absorbing chain, rather than a study-dropout event or the disease-process parameters that are the target in \cite{lange2015}. The conceptual relationship to shared-parameter and event-history models is close and I do not claim otherwise; what is added here is the combination of a standard discrete-time filter under state-dependent laboratory observation with the complete first-passage distribution, including its day-zero mass and finite-horizon tail, evaluated with censoring-aware proper scores; Section~\ref{sec:hiddenstate} contrasts this with an absorption-probability risk score from earlier disease-progression work.

Mechanically, the Bernoulli draw process is modeled on every day and enters the HMM emission likelihood alongside the wearable and observed laboratory factors, so information from a lab-absent day still updates the posterior through the $1-\lambda_i$ term.

\subsection{Hidden-state disease progression and first-passage prediction}\label{sec:hiddenstate}
Forward filtering, forward--backward smoothing, and Baum--Welch estimation are classical HMM methods \cite{rabiner,cappe}. Absorbing Markov-chain identities provide exact first-passage distributions and means through the transient block of the transition matrix and the associated fundamental matrix \cite{kemeny}. Joint longitudinal--event modeling connects evolving biomarkers to event times through shared structure \cite{tsiatis}, whereas threshold-regression work treats event times as first passage of a stochastic process to a boundary \cite{lee_whitmore}. Related disease-progression work has used hidden-state and semi-Markov models to represent clinical deterioration and absorption risk \cite{alaa} and continuous-time disease progression \cite{liu_cthmm}. Alaa \emph{et al.}~\cite{alaa} model clinical observation times as a marked Hawkes process modulated by the latent state, addressing the informative-observation phenomenon that motivates the $\lambda_i$ factor here, and compute a risk score that composes the forward-filtered state posterior with the probability of eventual absorption in the clinical-deterioration state, although, when scoring test episodes, they do not use observation times to infer the latent state. The present framework instead works in discrete time and targets the complete first-passage distribution, including its day-zero mass and finite-horizon tail, evaluated with censoring-aware proper scores.

\subsection{Evaluation of probabilistic event-time forecasts}
A predictive distribution should be evaluated by proper scoring rules, calibration, and sharpness rather than by discrimination alone. The Brier score has a standard survival-data extension~\cite{graf1999}, and Gneiting and Raftery provide the general theory of proper scores and restate the principle of maximizing sharpness subject to calibration \cite{gneiting2007}. Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) remains useful for the secondary question of current-state classification, but it does not evaluate the claimed time-to-flare distribution. Longitudinal wearable studies have also reported autonomic changes associated with ulcerative colitis activity \cite{hirten2021} and physiological changes preceding inflammatory bowel disease flares \cite{hirten2021,hirten2025}; those findings motivate the monitoring setting but do not constitute validation of the present simulation model.

\section{Model}
\subsection{Latent dynamics and observations}
Let $S_d\in\mathcal S=\{\R,\M,\F\}$ denote the latent disease state on day $d$, where $\R$, $\M$, and $\F$ denote Remission, Mild, and Flare. The physiological process is a time-homogeneous Markov chain with initial distribution $\vect{\eta}$ and transition matrix $P=(P_{ij})$, where $P_{ij}=\Prb(S_{d+1}=j\mid S_d=i)$. The flare state is not physiologically absorbing; it is made absorbing only within the landmark-specific first-passage calculation of Section~\ref{sec:hitting}.

Each day produces a vector $Y_d^{(w)}\in\mathbb R^p$ of daily channels, a laboratory-draw indicator $L_d\in\{0,1\}$, and, when $L_d=1$, a positive laboratory vector $Y_d^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb R_+^q$; here $p=q=3$ (Table~\ref{tab:emissions}). The three daily channels (temperature, resting heart rate, and a stool-frequency proxy) are called wearable channels for brevity, although stool frequency would in practice be patient-reported. The observed tuple is
\begin{equation}
O_d=\left(Y_d^{(w)},L_d,L_dY_d^{(\ell)}\right),
\end{equation}
where $L_dY_d^{(\ell)}$ denotes $Y_d^{(\ell)}$ when $L_d=1$ and is absent when $L_d=0$.
Days are indexed from zero: $d\in\{0,1,\ldots,D-1\}$ for a $D$-day observation window, and every observed day $d$ is a landmark, that is, a day on which a prediction is issued; the remaining observed follow-up at landmark $d$ is $C_d=D-1-d$. The filtration $\mathcal F_d=\sigma(O_0,\ldots,O_d)$ contains only information available through day $d$.

\subsection{Coherent observation likelihood}\label{sec:likelihood}
Conditional on $S_d=i$, the wearable channels are independent Gaussian summaries,
\begin{equation}
Y_d^{(w)}\mid S_d=i\sim\mathcal N(\vect{\mu}_i^{(w)},\operatorname{diag}((\vect{\sigma}_i^{(w)})^2)),
\end{equation}
the draw indicator is Bernoulli,
\begin{equation}
L_d\mid S_d=i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\lambda_i),
\end{equation}
and each positive laboratory channel is log-normal when observed,
\begin{equation}
\log Y_d^{(\ell)}\mid(S_d=i,L_d=1)
\sim\mathcal N(\vect{\nu}_i,\operatorname{diag}(\vect{\tau}_i^2)).
\end{equation}
Conditional on $S_d$, the wearable vector is independent of $(L_d,Y_d^{(\ell)})$, and $O_d$ is independent of the states and observations of all other days, so the complete day-$d$ emission factor is
\begin{align}
 b_i(O_d)&=\phi_p\!\left(Y_d^{(w)};\vect{\mu}_i^{(w)},\vect{\sigma}_i^{(w)}\right)
 \lambda_i^{L_d}(1-\lambda_i)^{1-L_d} \notag\\
 &\quad\times\left[f_{\mathrm{LN}}\!\left(Y_d^{(\ell)};\vect{\nu}_i,\vect{\tau}_i\right)\right]^{L_d}.
 \label{eq:emission}
\end{align}
Here $\phi_p$ denotes the product of $p$ univariate normal densities with means $\vect{\mu}_i^{(w)}$ and standard deviations $\vect{\sigma}_i^{(w)}$, and $f_{\mathrm{LN}}$ denotes the product of $q$ univariate log-normal densities whose logarithms have means $\vect{\nu}_i$ and standard deviations $\vect{\tau}_i$. Because the laboratory-density factor carries the exponent $L_d$, it equals one when $L_d=0$. Fig.~\ref{fig:graph} shows the resulting graphical structure. No forward-filled laboratory value is evaluated in the HMM likelihood. Last-observation-carried-forward values are used only as causal covariates in the separate event-history comparator.

\subsection{Filtering under endogenous sampling}
Let $\pi_d(i)=\Prb(S_d=i\mid\mathcal F_d)$ and $\pi_{d\mid d-1}(i)=\Prb(S_d=i\mid\mathcal F_{d-1})$, with $\mathcal F_{-1}$ trivial, so that $\pi_{0\mid -1}(i)=\eta_i$. The prediction step \eqref{eq:predict} applies for $d\ge 1$, and the update step \eqref{eq:update} applies from $d=0$:
\begin{align}
\pi_{d\mid d-1}(i)&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}\pi_{d-1}(j)P_{ji}\label{eq:predict},\\
\pi_d(i)&=\frac{b_i(O_d)\pi_{d\mid d-1}(i)}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal S}b_k(O_d)\pi_{d\mid d-1}(k)}\label{eq:update}.
\end{align}
On a lab-absent day, posterior odds between states $i$ and $j$ contain the factor
\begin{equation}
\frac{1-\lambda_i}{1-\lambda_j}.
\end{equation}
If $\lambda_i>\lambda_j$, then, for the same one-step predictive distribution $\pi_{d\mid d-1}$ and value-emission parameters, laboratory absence multiplies the posterior odds of state $i$ against state $j$ by a factor below one, relative to an update without the draw factor (Appendix~\ref{app:contraction}). When all $\lambda_i$ are equal, the factor cancels and the model reduces to the HMM without the draw factor.

\section{First-Passage Prediction}\label{sec:hitting}
At every landmark day $d$, define the rolling waiting time
\begin{equation}
H_d=\inf\{n\ge 0:S_{d+n}=\F\}.
\end{equation}
For the first-passage calculation, with the states ordered $(\R,\M,\F)$, replace the flare row by an absorbing row. Write
\begin{equation}
P^*=\begin{pmatrix}Q&\vect{r}\\\vect{0}^{\trans}&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad N=(I-Q)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Here $Q$ is the $2\times2$ transient block, $\vect{r}$ is the transient-to-flare column, $I$ is the identity matrix, $\rho(\cdot)$ denotes spectral radius, and $\vect{0}$ and $\vect{1}$ are vectors of zeros and ones. Assume $\rho(Q)<1$, so the flare state is eventually reached from either transient state and $N=(I-Q)^{-1}$ is well defined. Let
\begin{equation}
\vect{q}_d=(\pi_d(\R),\pi_d(\M))^{\trans}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Unconditional law}
From standard absorbing-chain identities~\cite{kemeny}, the complete landmark-specific distribution is
\begin{align}
\Prb(H_d=0\mid\mathcal F_d)&=\pi_d(\F),\label{eq:p0}\\
\Prb(H_d=n\mid\mathcal F_d)&=\vect{q}_d^{\trans}Q^{n-1}\vect{r},
\quad n\ge1,\label{eq:pn}\\
\Prb(H_d>h\mid\mathcal F_d)&=\vect{q}_d^{\trans}Q^h\vect{1},
\quad h\ge0.\label{eq:tail}
\end{align}
The final expression is retained as a tail category in every finite numerical PMF. In particular, the $D$-day observation window ($D=@@N_DAYS@@$; Section~\ref{sec:simulation}) is never used to renormalize the predictive distribution.

The unconditional mean is
\begin{equation}
\E(H_d\mid\mathcal F_d)=\vect{q}_d^{\trans}N\vect{1}.
\label{eq:uncondmean}
\end{equation}
This mean can approach zero because it is multiplied implicitly by the posterior probability of still being in a transient state (Fig.~\ref{fig:interface}, middle).

\subsection{Conditional law given no current flare}
When $\vect{q}_d^{\trans}\vect{1}>0$, the transient-conditional law is
\[
\Prb(H_d=n\mid\mathcal F_d,S_d\in\{\R,\M\})
=\frac{\vect{q}_d^{\trans}Q^{n-1}\vect{r}}{\vect{q}_d^{\trans}\vect{1}},\quad n\ge1,
\]
and its mean is
\begin{equation}
\E(H_d\mid\mathcal F_d,S_d\in\{\R,\M\})
=\frac{\vect{q}_d^{\trans}N\vect{1}}{\vect{q}_d^{\trans}\vect{1}}.
\label{eq:condmean}
\end{equation}
Let $m_{\R}=(N\vect{1})_{\R}$ and $m_{\M}=(N\vect{1})_{\M}$ denote the mean hitting times of the flare state from Remission and Mild. The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:condmean} is a convex combination of $m_{\R}$ and $m_{\M}$, so
\begin{align}
\min(m_{\R},m_{\M})
&\le \E(H_d\mid\mathcal F_d,S_d\in\{\R,\M\}) \notag\\
&\le\max(m_{\R},m_{\M}).
\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
Proofs of~\eqref{eq:uncondmean} and~\eqref{eq:bound} are given in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The conditional mean is mathematically undefined whenever the transient posterior mass is exactly zero. In the numerical implementation I additionally suppress the conditional mean and display it as a gap when the transient mass (for the parameter mixture, the member-averaged transient mass) falls at or below $10^{-8}$; by \eqref{eq:bound}, applied to each mixture member, the ratio itself remains bounded as the transient mass decreases, but when the patient is almost certainly in flare it carries no practical information. The choice between ``undefined'' and ``suppressed below threshold'' is definitional, not a change to the underlying joint prediction: the unconditional law $\Prb(H_d=n\mid\mathcal F_d)$ and the day-zero mass $\Prb(H_d=0\mid\mathcal F_d)$ are unaffected.

\subsection{Parameter-mixture predictive law}
For mixture members $\theta^{(0)},\ldots,\theta^{(B)}$, each a complete HMM parameter set (initial distribution, transition matrix, state-specific emission parameters and, for the endogenous-sampling (draw) model, the laboratory-draw probabilities), where $\theta^{(0)}$ is the EM estimate and $\theta^{(1)},\ldots,\theta^{(B)}$ are $B=@@N_PARAM_DRAWS@@$ parameter draws (Section~\ref{sec:paramunc}), the reported PMF is
\begin{equation}
\widehat p_d(n)=\frac{1}{B+1}\sum_{b=0}^{B}
 p_{\theta^{(b)}}(H_d=n\mid\mathcal F_d),
\end{equation}
with the filtering recursion and first-passage transformation recomputed for every mixture member. This can widen the predictive distribution when transition, emission, or sampling parameters are uncertain. The transient-conditional mean of the mixture is formed after marginalizing over mixture members, as the ratio of the member-averaged numerator and denominator of \eqref{eq:condmean}. The bound \eqref{eq:bound} holds for each member. Because the mixture mean is a weighted average of the member conditional means, with weights proportional to each member's transient posterior mass, it lies between the smallest and largest member hitting-time means; it need not lie between $m_{\R}$ and $m_{\M}$ of the EM estimate alone, and in the reported runs it often does not.

\section{Latent-State Estimation and Comparators}\label{sec:estimation}
\subsection{Baum--Welch estimation}
Every HMM (the proposed model and both HMM comparators) is fit without access to simulator state labels. For training patient $u$, the forward--backward recursions produce
\begin{align}
\gamma_{ud}(i)&=\Prb(S_{ud}=i\mid O_{u,0:D-1}),\\
\xi_{ud}(i,j)&=\Prb(S_{ud}=i,S_{u,d+1}=j\mid O_{u,0:D-1}).
\end{align}
Transition-count sums run over $d=0,\ldots,D-2$, whereas emission sums run over $d=0,\ldots,D-1$.

The principal M-step updates are
\begin{align}
\widehat P_{ij}&=
\frac{\sum_{u,d}\xi_{ud}(i,j)+\alpha_{ij}}
{\sum_{u,d}\sum_k\xi_{ud}(i,k)+\sum_k \alpha_{ik}},\label{eq:Pupdate}\\
\widehat\lambda_i&=
\frac{\sum_{u,d}\gamma_{ud}(i)L_{ud}+\alpha_i}
{\sum_{u,d}\gamma_{ud}(i)+\alpha_i+\beta_i},\label{eq:lamupdate}\\
\widehat{\vect{\nu}}_i&=
\frac{\sum_{u,d}\gamma_{ud}(i)L_{ud}\log Y_{ud}^{(\ell)}}
{\sum_{u,d}\gamma_{ud}(i)L_{ud}},\label{eq:labupdate}
\end{align}
with analogous responsibility-weighted updates for Gaussian wearable means and variances and log-laboratory variances. Weak pseudocounts ($\alpha_{ij}=0.5$ for transitions and $\alpha_i=\beta_i=1$ for draw rates) prevent zero transition and draw probabilities. Initialization uses $k$\nobreakdash-means on standardized training wearables; labels are re-sorted after every EM iteration and every parameter draw by increasing wearable-severity score (the mean, over channels, of each state's fitted wearable means standardized across states; for the draw-stratified-emission HMM, the means are first averaged over the two draw strata), not by true states. Among the $k$\nobreakdash-means starts (Section~\ref{sec:paramunc}), the fit with the highest final observed-data log likelihood is retained. The M-step also uses small numerical safeguards: a 0.5 pseudocount on the initial distribution, standard-deviation floors, laboratory and draw-stratified wearable updates only when the corresponding total responsibility exceeds 0.2, and draw rates clipped to $[10^{-4},1-10^{-4}]$. Because of these pseudocounts and safeguards, the procedure is a lightly penalized, safeguarded variant of EM. The observed-data log likelihood determines convergence: iteration stops when its change is at most @@EM_TOL@@ times one plus the absolute value of the previous log likelihood, or after @@MAX_EM_ITER@@ iterations; at least three iterations are run.

\subsection{Parameter uncertainty}\label{sec:paramunc}
At the final EM solution, responsibility-weighted expected counts are used to construct a reproducible empirical-Bayes approximation. Initial and transition probabilities are drawn from Dirichlet distributions based on expected counts; laboratory-draw rates are sampled from beta distributions based on expected draw-day and non-draw-day counts; channel-wise independent Gaussian/log-Gaussian means and scales are sampled from normal--scaled-inverse-$\chi^2$ approximations weakly regularized toward the EM estimate. @@N_PARAM_DRAWS_WORD_CAP@@ draws plus the EM estimate are used for each HMM, for @@N_PARAM_MEMBERS@@ mixture members per model. Each base EM fit uses @@N_STARTS_WORD@@ $k$\nobreakdash-means initializations, which differ only in the $k$\nobreakdash-means random seed, and retains every likelihood trace and selection diagnostic. This procedure propagates parameter variation but conditions on the final EM responsibilities; it is therefore not claimed to be an exact fully Bayesian posterior.

\subsection{Comparators}
Four models produce a PMF over the same $H_d$ outcome.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{HMM + draw model} (the proposed endogenous-sampling HMM, also called the draw model): emission factor \eqref{eq:emission}, including $\lambda_i^{L_d}(1-\lambda_i)^{1-L_d}$.
\item \emph{HMM without draw model} (the no-draw HMM): the same latent dynamics and value emissions, but the Bernoulli draw factor is omitted.
\item \emph{Draw-stratified-emission HMM}: wearable emission parameters are stratified by the currently observed draw status $L_d=0$ versus $L_d=1$, whereas one transition matrix and one filtered posterior are retained. As in the no-draw HMM, the Bernoulli draw factor is omitted, so, beyond determining whether a laboratory density is evaluated (as in every HMM here), draw status enters only through the stratified wearable parameters. This is a deliberately narrow pattern-stratified comparator rather than a claim to represent every pattern-mixture formulation. Because the required posterior and transition matrix exist, \eqref{eq:p0}--\eqref{eq:tail} are applied directly.
\item \emph{Discrete-time event-history model}: a supervised comparator with two logistic components, both fit on training-set landmarks, taken every @@HAZARD_STRIDE_ORD@@ day (to bound the size of the person-period data set), with ground-truth flare labels. Hazard training rows use only latent states inside the @@N_DAYS@@-day observation window: at landmark $d$ they cover lead days $n\le C_d=D-1-d$ (the training-horizon scale $n_{\mathrm{train}}$ defined below does not bind here) and are right-censored at the end of the window, so the additional latent days are never used for fitting. Both components share the landmark covariate vector $x_d$, consisting of current wearables, wearable means and slopes over the most recent seven days (fewer before day~6), the draw indicator, last observed log-laboratory values, time since the last panel (capped at 60 days and divided by 60), and an explicit indicator that a first laboratory panel has become available. The hazard input additionally uses the lead-time basis defined below; the hazard logit is linear in the standardized $x_d$ and the basis terms, with no covariate-by-lead-time interactions. Before a patient's first panel, log-laboratory features use the training-cohort median (the median over all laboratory panels in the training cohort), and time since the last panel is set to its cap. The covariates in $x_d$, but not the lead-time basis, are standardized with means and standard deviations computed over the pooled training rows of both components (each sampled landmark together with its lead-day hazard rows). Both logistic components are $\ell_2$-regularized (inverse regularization strength 1.0 for the current-state logistic and 0.5 for the hazard logistic). The current-state logistic predicts the probability $g_0(x_d)$ that the patient is in flare on the current day. The hazard logistic predicts a first flare at lead day $n$, conditional on remaining flare-free through lead day $n-1$ and not being in flare at the landmark:
\[
g(x_d, n) = \Prb(H_d = n \mid H_d \ge n,\ S_d \neq \F,\ x_d).
\]
Let $n_{\mathrm{train}} = @@HAZARD_HORIZON@@$ denote the training-horizon scale and $n_{\max} = @@PMF_HORIZON@@$ the prediction-grid horizon. The hazard time basis is $\bigl(\log(1+n),\ \sqrt{n}/10,\ n/n_{\mathrm{train}}\bigr)$; it is extrapolated beyond the longest lead represented in training (at most $D-1$ days; long leads are sparse because hazard rows stop at the first flare) up to $n_{\max}$. For each ensemble member $b$, with components $g_0$ and $g$, and $1 \le n \le n_{\max}$,
\[
\widehat p^{(b)}_d(0) = g_0(x_d),
\]
\[
\widehat p^{(b)}_d(n) = [1 - g_0(x_d)]\, g(x_d, n) \prod_{m=1}^{n-1}[1 - g(x_d, m)],
\]
\[
\widehat p^{(b)}_d({>}\,n_{\max}) = [1 - g_0(x_d)] \prod_{m=1}^{n_{\max}}[1 - g(x_d, m)].
\]
Hazards are clipped to $[10^{-5}, 1-10^{-5}]$; by construction each row sums to one over the tail-inclusive grid, and a final normalization only removes floating-point rounding. The base fit is accompanied by @@N_HAZARD_DRAWS@@ patient-bootstrap refits of both components, giving @@N_HAZARD_MEMBERS@@ equally weighted members; the reported PMF $\widehat p_d$ is the arithmetic mean of the member PMFs, as for the HMM mixtures. Only information available through day $d$ enters prediction; training labels are used only in fitting, never at inference.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Censoring-aware evaluation}\label{sec:censoring}
For landmark $d$, let $C_d=D-1-d$ be the remaining observed follow-up. If a flare is observed after $h_d\le C_d$ days, the censored logarithmic score is $-\log\widehat p_d(h_d)$. If no flare is observed before administrative censoring, the score is
\begin{equation}
-\log\Bigl[1-\sum_{n=0}^{C_d}\widehat p_d(n)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
that is, minus the log of the predicted probability of no flare within the remaining $C_d$ days of follow-up (the unrenormalized survival tail). This right-censored log score is proper when censoring is independent of the event time~\cite{rindt2022}, as administrative censoring is here. Thus, patients without a flare inside @@N_DAYS@@ days remain in the analysis.

For $h\in\{7,14,30,60\}$, the fixed-horizon Brier score uses landmarks with complete $h$-day follow-up,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{BS}(h)=\frac1{|\mathcal D_h|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_h}
\left[\widehat F_d(h)-\mathbb I\{H_d\le h\}\right]^2,
\end{equation}
where $\widehat F_d(h)=\sum_{n=0}^{h}\widehat p_d(n)$ is the predicted probability of a flare within $h$ days, $\mathbb I\{\cdot\}$ is the indicator function, and $\mathcal D_h$ is the set of the patient's landmarks with $C_d\ge h$; the score is computed for each patient and then averaged across patients. The reported four-horizon mean is the average of the four Brier scores. Thirty-day calibration is displayed graphically and summarized by the slope of a logistic regression of the observed 30-day outcome on the logit of the predicted risk, with an intercept~\cite{cox1958}; predicted risks are clipped to $[10^{-5},1-10^{-5}]$, and a negligible ridge penalty stabilizes the fit numerically. Slope confidence intervals use the hierarchical seed--patient bootstrap. The calibration rows include current-flare landmarks, whose observed 30-day outcome is a flare by definition and whose predicted risks are near one; slopes excluding them are given below for description only. Equal-tailed 90\% interval coverage is assessed against each patient's simulated latent path from the landmark onward (the remaining observation-window states followed by the additional latent days), with the ${>}@@PMF_HORIZON@@$ tail retained as an observed outcome category; predictive entropy, computed over the categories $\{0,\ldots,@@PMF_HORIZON@@,{>}@@PMF_HORIZON@@\}$, is the primary sharpness summary. Log scores and entropy use natural logarithms. All @@N_DAYS@@ observed days of every test patient are landmarks, and each patient's scores average that patient's landmarks. AUROC for current flare status is secondary.

For performance intervals, the bootstrap first resamples seeds and then resamples patients within selected seeds, preserving every repeated landmark for a selected patient. Paired model differences are calculated at matched seed--patient pairs before the same hierarchical bootstrap. Because patients are resampled within resampled seeds, this two-stage bootstrap counts within-seed patient variation at both stages; for the primary paired comparison it gives a wider interval than a seed-level 95\% $t$ interval (Student $t$ with @@N_SEEDS_DF_WORD@@ degrees of freedom applied to the @@N_SEEDS_WORD@@ seed means of the patient-level paired differences), which is also reported (Section~\ref{sec:direct}). With only @@N_SEEDS_WORD@@ seeds, the seed-level interval is itself imprecise; in this run the seed means vary less than within-seed patient variation alone would predict, so it is probably somewhat narrower than the true uncertainty. No adjustment for multiple comparisons is applied. The main performance intervals use @@PERFORMANCE_BOOTSTRAP@@ replicates; calibration intervals use @@CALIBRATION_BOOTSTRAP@@ replicates. The primary estimand includes every landmark, including a current flare represented by the explicit day-zero mass. Of the test landmarks, @@CURRENT_FLARE_FRACTION@@ are current-flare landmarks. Scores restricted to landmarks without a current flare are not used for inference: selecting landmarks on the realized current state, which is part of the predicted outcome, makes the restricted log and Brier scores improper for the unconditional law. For description only, the archive contains this restricted analysis: there the no-draw-minus-draw log-score difference is @@NONCURRENT_DIFF_NLL@@ (@@NONCURRENT_DIFF_NLL_CI@@), and the 30-day calibration slopes are @@NCCAL_PROP@@ for the draw model and @@NCCAL_HAZ@@ for the event-history model.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Under the chosen state separability and draw rates, the explicit draw-indicator factor yielded at most a very small improvement over the otherwise identical HMM. The log-score difference is borderline: a seed-level $t$ interval excludes zero, but the hierarchical bootstrap interval does not, and no mean Brier difference is detected under either. This outcome is largely expected in the simulated regime: for every ordered pair of states, the per-day Kullback--Leibler divergence between the generating wearable emissions is @@KL_RATIO_MIN@@ to @@KL_RATIO_MAX@@ times that between the corresponding draw-indicator laws, so the wearable channels identify the state far more strongly than the timing of laboratory panels does. The regime is therefore a near-null setting for the draw indicator: the upper 95\% confidence limit of the log-score difference, @@NULL_NLL_UB@@, excludes a relative gain larger than about @@NULL_GM_PCT@@ in the geometric mean of the predictive probability assigned to the observed outcome. The descriptive restriction to landmarks without a current flare (Section~\ref{sec:censoring}), which is not used for inference, gives a larger but still small difference of @@NONCURRENT_DIFF_NLL@@. The posterior-odds contraction on lab-absent days (Appendix~\ref{app:contraction}) does not by itself imply a practically important gain in proper predictive scores. The gain may be larger when value emissions overlap more strongly, sampling contrasts are more extreme, or draw timing contains information not already captured by the wearable channels. Those regimes should be pre-specified and examined in a separate sensitivity study rather than inferred from the present experiment.

The draw-stratified-emission HMM also produces a legitimate time-to-flare PMF in the implemented form because it retains one transition matrix and one state posterior. It conditions only the wearable emissions on the observed draw stratum and is not a general pattern-mixture model. Its proper scores were close to those of the proposed model (Table~\ref{tab:paired}), and its predictive entropy was slightly lower (Table~\ref{tab:mainresults}). The event-history comparator shows why classification AUROC alone is insufficient: its current-state AUROC, @@HAZ_AUROC@@, was also near the ceiling, yet its proper scores were clearly worse. That comparator is supervised and structurally different, so its weaker performance in this simulation does not establish a general ranking. Broader studies should include joint longitudinal--survival models and latent Markov models for informative dropout such as those in \cite{bartolucci2015,bartolucci2019,marino2020}.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
The study is simulation-only, and the principal HMM is correctly specified. State emissions are highly separated, producing near-ceiling state AUROC and limiting the realism of the classification task. The generating chain is flare-rich (stationary Flare probability @@STAT_FLARE@@), so absolute risks should not be read as clinical flare frequencies, and calibration results may not transfer to lower-risk settings. Only one Markov transition regime and one set of draw rates are used, and @@N_SEEDS_WORD@@ simulation seeds provide only a modest number of top-level uncertainty clusters. EM can converge to local optima. In every fit, the @@N_STARTS_WORD@@ $k$\nobreakdash-means starts reached final log likelihoods within @@START_SPREAD_BOUND@@ of one another; because the starts differ only in the $k$\nobreakdash-means random seed, this agreement is only weak evidence that EM avoided a poor local optimum. Every EM fit met the stated relative stopping rule after @@EM_ITER_MIN@@ to @@EM_ITER_MAX@@ iterations, well before the @@MAX_EM_ITER@@-iteration cap. The archived traces show little remaining optimization: in every reported fit each log-likelihood increase was positive and at most @@EM_RATIO_MAX@@ times the previous one, the final update added at most @@EM_LAST_GAIN_MAX@@, and geometric extrapolation at each fit's largest observed ratio suggests at most about @@EM_REMAIN_MAX@@ more, against absolute final log likelihoods of about $@@EM_ABS_LL_MIN@@\times 10^{4}$ to $@@EM_ABS_LL_MAX@@\times 10^{4}$. The responsibility-conditioned empirical-Bayes mixture is not a fully Bayesian posterior because state responsibilities are not redrawn jointly with parameters. The event-history uncertainty ensemble resamples training patients and refits both logistic components, but it remains a model-specific bootstrap rather than a joint Bayesian analysis. Equal-tailed 90\% intervals for a discrete predictive law contain at least 90\% predictive probability, so empirical coverage can exceed the nominal level (Section~\ref{sec:primary}). The fixed-horizon Brier analysis for all models uses landmarks with complete follow-up at the relevant horizon. The four-horizon mean therefore averages scores over horizon-specific eligible populations. Random censoring would require inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting~\cite{graf1999} or another censoring model. Finally, latent future continuation is available only because the data are simulated; real clinical calibration requires prospectively observed flare outcomes.

\subsection{Deferred external validation}
A real-data evaluation should be undertaken only after four conditions are met: individual-level data and analysis code are available so that every reported count can be reproduced; repeated observations are analyzed with patient-clustered or mixed-effects methods; the transition matrix is estimated on the actual observation cadence rather than at a daily resolution imposed on visit-scale data; and the flare outcome is independently adjudicated and is not defined using the same measurement serving as the principal predictor. In many research cohorts laboratory draws are scheduled by the study protocol; such draws should not be interpreted as symptom-triggered clinician ordering without additional evidence. Until these conditions are met, external data may motivate the model but cannot validate the time-to-flare PMF.

\section{Conclusion}
A coherent hidden Markov model with endogenous laboratory sampling can generate a complete first-passage distribution at every landmark day. The clinically interpretable output requires an explicit day-zero mass, an unrenormalized survival tail, and a clear distinction between unconditional and transient-conditional means. When the model is fit without oracle states and evaluated with censoring-aware proper scores, the simulation study shows that the interface can be estimated and evaluated end to end but finds no practically important predictive benefit from the laboratory-draw indicator in the tested regime. The appropriate next step is independent reproduction of the tagged pipeline, followed by pre-specified robustness experiments and prospective clinical validation.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\break}{}Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
\subsection{First-passage distribution and mean}
For $n\ge1$, a first hit at $d+n$ requires the chain to remain transient for $n-1$ steps and move to flare on the next step, giving $\vect{q}_d^{\trans}Q^{n-1}\vect{r}$. The probability of no hit through $h$ steps is $\vect{q}_d^{\trans}Q^h\vect{1}$. Summing the tail probabilities and using the Neumann series,
\begin{align}
\E(H_d\mid\mathcal F_d)
&=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Prb(H_d>h\mid\mathcal F_d)\notag\\
&=\vect{q}_d^{\trans}\left(\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}Q^h\right)\vect{1}
=\vect{q}_d^{\trans}(I-Q)^{-1}\vect{1}.
\end{align}
The day-zero mass contributes zero to the mean but reduces $\vect{q}_d^{\trans}\vect{1}$, allowing the unconditional mean to approach zero.

\subsection{Transient-conditional bound}
Let $\omega_i=\pi_d(i)/(\pi_d(\R)+\pi_d(\M))$ for $i\in\{\R,\M\}$. Then $\omega_i\ge0$, $\omega_{\R}+\omega_{\M}=1$, and \eqref{eq:condmean} equals $\omega_{\R}m_{\R}+\omega_{\M}m_{\M}$, proving \eqref{eq:bound}.

\subsection{Posterior-odds contraction on a lab-absent day}\label{app:contraction}
When $L_d=0$, the emission factor \eqref{eq:emission} and the update step \eqref{eq:update} give
\begin{equation}
\frac{\pi_d(i)}{\pi_d(j)}=
\frac{1-\lambda_i}{1-\lambda_j}
\frac{\phi_p(Y_d^{(w)};\vect{\mu}_i^{(w)},\vect{\sigma}_i^{(w)})}
{\phi_p(Y_d^{(w)};\vect{\mu}_j^{(w)},\vect{\sigma}_j^{(w)})}
\frac{\pi_{d\mid d-1}(i)}{\pi_{d\mid d-1}(j)}.
\end{equation}
Holding the remaining parameters and the predictive prior fixed, omitting the draw factor removes the first ratio. Thus, if $1>\lambda_i>\lambda_j$, endogenous sampling contracts the posterior odds for $i$ relative to $j$ by a factor strictly between zero and one.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\break}{}Acceptance Tests}\label{app:tests}
Item 1 is checked by unit tests for the HMMs and by the verification script for the archived PMFs of all four models; items 11 and 12 by unit tests on small synthetic inputs and by the verification script on the archived outputs; items 8 and 10 by the verification script; and items 2--7 and 9 only by unit tests (for the calibration intervals in item 9, the resampling unit is fixed by construction).
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every landmark and model, the PMF, including the ${>}@@PMF_HORIZON@@$ tail category, sums to one to numerical tolerance (each mixture member's row is divided by its total, which before that step differs from one only by floating-point rounding).
\item For each member of an HMM parameter mixture, the conditional mean is either suppressed (transient mass at most $10^{-8}$) or lies between the two state-specific means.
\item Changing a stored laboratory value on a day with $L_d=0$ leaves the HMM likelihood unchanged.
\item The simulator draws a new wearable error vector on every patient-day.
\item Training, validation, and test patient identifiers are disjoint.
\item HMM estimation uses only wearable values, laboratory values when drawn, and draw indicators. Simulator state arrays are not read by HMM initialization, estimation, or filtering. They are used only for evaluation (outcomes, descriptive breakdowns by current state, and the exemplar patient and its flare-entry days in Fig.~\ref{fig:interface}) and to train the separately identified supervised event-history comparator.
\item Censored log scores use the survival tail and retain patients with no observed flare.
\item Draw-stratified-emission and no-draw HMMs are transformed to the same time-to-flare outcome as the proposed HMM.
\item Hierarchical confidence intervals resample seeds, then patients within seeds; by construction, each resampled unit is a patient-level summary (for calibration, all of a patient's landmark rows), never a patient-day row.
\item Generated tables agree with the manuscript to the displayed precision when regenerated by the verification script from the archived patient- and landmark-level files.
\item The event-history pre-panel laboratory reference is computed from training data and accompanied by an explicit panel-history indicator; no generating laboratory parameter enters the comparator.
\item Every seed archives a complete PMF tensor, every fitted model object, the likelihood traces and selection diagnostics of the selected and non-selected EM starts, and all patient-bootstrap event-history members.
\end{enumerate}
